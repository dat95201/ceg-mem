\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers come from the frozen log via the extractors in the artifact
(\cref{sec:availability}); $p$-values are exact two-sided sign tests. Five
seeds of one task share a fault, a model and a cache, so the cell is not an
independent replication: \cref{tab:main} carries every headline comparison at
both units with a task-clustered bootstrap interval, and the task-level result
carries the claim. The confirmatory family is repair at a matched oracle budget
and oracle calls, on the three main arms; everything else is exploratory.

\subsection{RQ1: what memory buys}
\label{sec:results-cost}

\input{tables/main}

Over all \num{495} paired cells (\cref{tab:main}), memory cuts oracle calls per
episode from $9.47$ to $1.91$ (untyped) and $1.83$ (typed)---\fold{4.95} and
\fold{5.17}---and \emph{paid} redundant verifications, where a proposal whose
class was already refuted still reaches the oracle, from $4.48$ to $0.101$ and
$0.079$. Repair rate is unchanged ($0.703$, $0.699$, $0.697$; flips $36/37$,
\pval{1.0}), so this is not accuracy traded for cost.

\paragraph{The guard executes, and the accounting says so.}
A guard that declines a validation still runs a stored counterexample, so a
comparison denominated only in oracle calls would hide its cost. Ours does not:
the execution counter bills every guard replay in every arm. Counting that way,
total program executions still fall from $95.68$ to $43.01$, \fold{2.22}, on
$251$ of $364$ discordant cells and $63$ of $93$ discordant tasks
(\pval{0.0008}), with the guard's share of the bill at $22\%$. The split is
conservative against the memory arms: a consultation is billed as an execution
even when deduplication or memoization means no process started. Per round the
trade is legible, and it is an ordering trade (\cref{sec:related-order}): in
\armtyped\ a round the guard resolves costs $1.13$ executions against $18.9$
for a round that reaches the oracle. Those surviving calls are dearer than no
memory's ($12.5$) because the cheap refutations are exactly the ones the guard
reaches first---a selection effect that flatters neither arm, since both totals
are reported. Typing does
\emph{not} reduce total executions relative to a flat log ($44.45$ vs.\
$43.01$; $170/146$, \pval{0.20}); what it reduces is the time those
consultations take (\cref{sec:results-ablation}).

\paragraph{The primary metric.}
\Cref{fig:budget} gives repair rate against a matched oracle-call budget. The
arms coincide at $b = 1$ up to the timeout-edge cell. At $b = 2$ they
separate---$31.9\%$
(no memory) against $51.3\%$ (untyped) and $55.8\%$ (typed)---and the memory
arms are within $0.3$\,pp of their asymptote by $b = 4$, where no memory has
reached $43.8\%$. No memory needs the full $b = 20$ to reach $70.3\%$;
\armguard\ reaches $70.7\%$ at $b = 4$. The gain survives the conservative
unit: the task-clustered interval on the paired difference at $b = 2$ is
$[18.4, 29.5]$\,pp.

Counted in \emph{proposals} rather than oracle calls the arms nearly coincide
($0.564$ / $0.560$ / $0.578$ at eight charged proposals), exactly as
\cref{cor:budget} predicts
when blocked rounds are charged: memory converts the expensive resource into
repairs, it does not make the model smarter. The saving scales with difficulty,
\fold{12.1} in \emph{dead} to \fold{1.2} in \emph{too-easy}.

\textbf{RQ1.} Memory converts verification budget into repairs: \fold{5.2}
fewer oracle calls, \fold{2.2} fewer program executions counting guard replays,
and \pp{+23.8} repair at a two-call budget, at unchanged repair rate.

\subsection{RQ2: does typing beat a flat log?}
\label{sec:results-typing}

Typed uses fewer oracle calls than untyped on $93$ of $152$ discordant cells
(\pval{0.007}, $\Atwelve = 0.524$) and on $36$ of $55$ discordant tasks
(\pval{0.030}; signed-rank \pval{0.023}), with a clustered interval on the
paired difference of $[-0.141, -0.018]$ calls. Pooled over the three primary
bands the margin is $71/39$ (\pval{0.0029}). The task-level version is the
claim: real, signed, and small---$1.83$ against $1.91$ calls.

One caveat: \armtyped\ differs from \armuntyped\ in two ways at once---indexing
the guard by $\loc$ \emph{and} putting exclusion blocks in the prompt---so the
difference above is the package's, and \cref{sec:results-ablation} separates
them.

\paragraph{Is it the types, or just partitioning?}
Any typed index invites the objection that \emph{any} partition would do. The
pre-specified control files each refutation under a location drawn uniformly
from those seen so far: memory still partitions and the guard is still indexed,
but the partition carries no information about failure class. On the same
\num{90} paired sweep cells exactly one quantity responds---redundant proposals
the guard \emph{recognizes}, $4.80$ at $\coh = 1.0$ against $4.04$ at
$\coh = 0$ and $4.47$ for the control, correct typing winning on $9/9$
discordant cells (\pval{0.004}). Nothing else does: paid redundancy ties on all
$90$, and guard seconds, entries consulted, oracle calls and repair rate all
give $p > 0.6$. The bucket-only resolution rate is flat in $\coh$
($33.1$--$35.3\%$) and the control sits inside that range: an uninformative
partition still \emph{fills} buckets, so the first guess lands on a non-empty
one about a third of the time either way.

\textbf{RQ2.} The licensed claim is narrower than ``typing helps'': typing
determines \emph{which} redundant proposals the guard recognizes, does not
affect blocking correctness (guaranteed by the fallback scan,
\cref{thm:sound}), and on this sweep does not measurably affect guard time.

\subsection{RQ3: which operation does the work?}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

\input{tables/ablation}

\paragraph{The guard: identical outcomes, cheaper answers.}
\armguard\ and \armuntyped\ share their draws, so they should produce identical
episodes and differ only in bookkeeping---and they do, on $295$ of $297$ cells,
the two exceptions being the timeout-edge task (\cref{sec:results-integrity}).
The indexed arm repairs $0.707$ against $0.704$ and consults $9.74$ against
$10.02$ ($19/13$, \pval{0.38}), while guard seconds fall $6.66 \to 4.04$ on
$191$ of the $231$ episodes where the guard ran (\plt{10^{-24}}). The
\emph{sign} is solid and the \emph{magnitude} is not: the task-clustered
interval is $[-6.5, -0.5]$\,s and one fault supplies \SI{1.8}{\second} of the
baseline (\appref{app:sensitivity}{the supplement}). Holding the episode fixed
isolates \Rguardcost---the same work, answered faster---but the $35\%$ of
decisions the $\loc$ bucket settles is a rate the random-partition control also
reaches, so the clock is recording deduplication, not the index.

\paragraph{Steering: null on every outcome, and expensive.}
\armsteer\ keeps the prompt blocks and drops the guard. Round~1 matches no
memory on all $297$ cells and trajectories diverge where the first evidence
block enters; after that nothing moves: repair $0.707 \to 0.727$
($23/17$, \pval{0.43}), oracle calls $9.45 \to 8.81$ ($80/71$, \pval{0.52}),
calls to first accept $5.08 \to 4.62$, paid redundancy $4.43 \to 4.16$. What
moves is the bill: prompt tokens $4519 \to \num{10296}$, $+128\%$ ($64/170$,
\plt{10^{-11}}), growing $+5.9$ per round against untyped's $-1.2$.

The mechanism-level null is sharper than the outcome-level one. Length-matched
by failed-round position, the rate at which the proposer re-draws an
\emph{already-eliminated} class is $50.2\%$ without the exclusion block and
$50.7\%$ with it. The model conditions on the block---trajectories shift
immediately---but does not avoid the classes it names.
\Cref{eq:steer}'s renormalization is requested and not granted.

The consequence is an ordering on the clock. Over the $297$ shared cells:
\armguard\ \SI{68.9}{\second} per episode, \armuntyped\ $80.4$, \armsteer\
$98.2$, \armnomem\ $110.8$, \armtyped\ $155.0$. On a local 7B prompt length is
latency, so typed's prompt tokens become $+93\%$ wall-clock over untyped on
these cells, and $+81\%$ on the main grid, where the paired test is
$224$ wins to $270$ (\pval{0.043}). The arm that dominates every other on
wall-clock, oracle calls and tokens at once is \armguard---the index without
the prompt blocks.

\paragraph{Except in one band.}
Steering is not uniformly worthless. In the \emph{dead} band---per-round success
under $2\%$---repair rises from $2.6\%$ (no memory and untyped, identical) to
$12.2\%$ (typed) on the main grid (per task \pval{0.047}), and the ablation
decomposes it as \armguard\ $4.3\%$, \armsteer\ $10.1\%$, \armtyped\ $14.5\%$.
Concrete evidence in the prompt, not the guard, is what rescues episodes where
nothing else succeeds. This is one band of five at a threshold $p$-value and we
have not tested the interaction, so we treat it as a hypothesis about where
steering could pay (\cref{sec:threats}).

\textbf{RQ3.} The guard carries the value---identical episodes, $39\%$ cheaper
guard time, $14\%$ less wall-clock than untyped, no token overhead---while
prompt-side steering moves no outcome metric at $+128\%$ prompt tokens.
\armguard\ is the configuration to deploy.

\subsection{RQ4: is an accepted patch a repair?}
\label{sec:results-depth}

Every accepted patch is re-scored against the whole shipped pool, split into
the cases the faulty version fails and the cases it passes. Because $\Xk$
exhausts that pool for $97$ of the $99$ faults at $\oracledepth = 100$
(\cref{sec:arch}), acceptance there usually already \emph{is} pool adequacy:
all \num{1256} audited acceptances pass with $0$ overfitting, and $0$ of the
\num{939} carrying a regression verdict break a
case the buggy version passed---the denominators differ because a regression
verdict needs at least one such case and $317$ acceptances belong to faults
with none. A planted-mutant study finds $249/249$ non-equivalent mutants caught
($48$ of $297$ plants were equivalent). Three zero counts are not three zero
rates: the one-sided $95\%$ upper limits are $0.24\%$, $0.32\%$ and $1.2\%$.
What this establishes is membership in $\Gpool$, not $\Gstar$.

Sweeping the depth shows why that matters. Raw acceptance \emph{rises} as the
oracle gets shallower---$0.678$ at $\oracledepth = 100$ to $0.867$ at
$\oracledepth = 3$, and the audit says why: overfitting rises $0\%$, $7.6\%$,
$20.8\%$, $39.7\%$ as the depth falls $100, 20, 8, 3$. Full-pool repair is
monotone in $\oracledepth$, with \emph{no difference} between the two deepest
($0.678$ at both $20$ and $100$, at $55\%$ of the executions) while
$\oracledepth \le 8$ is unsafe ($0.633$, then $0.522$); we cannot locate a
saturation point between $20$ and $100$. At $\oracledepth \le 20$, $\Xk$ is a
proper subset of most pools, so this is also where \cref{thm:sound}'s second
clause is unaudited: a guarded arm there could lose a repair to an unsound
block and we cannot rule it out.

\textbf{RQ4.} At the reported depth acceptance means pool adequacy; a five-fold
shallower oracle preserves it, and at $\oracledepth = 3$ two of five
acceptances are false.

\subsection{RQ5: assumptions, and what steering costs when it misfires}
\label{sec:results-assumptions}

\paragraph{Informativeness measured, coherence not.}
An offline study re-executes stored counterexamples against other candidates of
the same label: the cross-refutation rate is $0.908$ ($95\%$ CI
$[0.884, 0.929]$) at fine granularity and $0.822$ $[0.790, 0.852]$ at coarse,
over \num{26048} executions. This estimates $\info$---how far a counterexample
generalizes---which is what the guard's usefulness depends on. It is \emph{not}
an estimate of $\coh$, the rate at which memory attributes a candidate to its
true class; that needs a ground-truth labelling the corpus does not supply, so
we perturb $\coh$ directly instead (\cref{sec:results-typing}). Earlier drafts
called this a coherence check; it is not, and only $\info$ is validated here.

\paragraph{The theory is directional, not calibrated.}
Fitting \Rredund's no-memory form across $79$ tasks gives $r = 0.657$ with
relative MAE $1.33$, an order-of-magnitude prediction the data respects in
shape; the memory-arm form could not be fit at all, its predicted spread being
smaller than the sampling noise. Their
i.i.d.\ assumption is what a real proposer violates, and the violation is the
redundancy a guard exists to intercept (\cref{sec:related-cegis}).

\paragraph{Anchoring: the cost of excluding a class.}
Typed steering can exclude the class the \emph{correct} fix belongs to. Over
\num{1503} steered episodes at $\coh = 1.0$ the correct patch's edit location
had entered the exclusion block in $5.7\%$ of episodes that then failed, and
the rate is highest in exactly the band where steering helps most ($19.7\%$ in
\emph{dead}, $0$ in the two easiest). Steering is a high-variance intervention
worth making only where the baseline is near zero.

\subsection{Free guarded rounds: the budget corollary, tested}
\label{sec:results-e9}

Under the reported accounting a blocked round costs a unit of budget, so
\cref{cor:budget} makes the guard outcome-neutral and its first half
untestable. Charging a blocked round a model call but no unit of the proposal
budget, both memory arms gain repair at unchanged oracle cost (untyped
$0.772 \to 0.860$, typed $0.815 \to 0.889$, paid for in proposals and tokens)
while the no-memory control, having no guard, is inert ($0.800 \to 0.783$).
Flips are $9/0$ pooled (\pval{0.004}), suggestive rather than confirmatory
because the arms share tasks and the condition completed on $171$ of a targeted
$180$ matched pairs. This is \cref{eq:steer}'s renormalization arriving on the
guard side, where the prompt could not deliver it.

\subsection{Integrity of the comparison}
\label{sec:results-integrity}

Common random numbers hold exactly: round~1 is byte-identical to the no-memory
arm on $495/495$ main-grid and $297/297$ ablation cells in all four treatment
arms, the frozen grid has $0$ missing cells of \num{1485}, and the three
pre-specified falsifiers behave. A single fault whose candidates run at the
\SI{30}{\second} sandbox edge produces every cross-arm inconsistency in the
study, including the two non-identical guard-only episodes. We left the frozen
timeout unchanged and repeat the headline
numbers without it in \appref{app:sensitivity}{the supplement}, where none
moves.

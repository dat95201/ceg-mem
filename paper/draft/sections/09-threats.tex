\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Our primary metric counts \emph{oracle calls}, the resource recent measurement
identifies as squandered~\cite{lin2026run,chen2026rethinking}. It is not the
only cost model---an agent billed per token, or one whose suite is cheap, would
rank the arms differently, and our own wall-clock row shows the full typed
agent losing under a latency-weighted one---so we report oracle calls, program
executions, tokens and wall-clock separately and name the resource each claim
is about. Guard replays are billed as executions and we claim no avoided
execution: a full $\Xk$ validation is replaced by one that terminates on its
first case, an ordering effect (\cref{sec:related-order}) that survives the
accounting. And ``repair'' is acceptance, a claim about $\Gk$ rather than a
fact about $\Gstar$; we audit it against $\Gpool$ with exact bounds, and no
test-based procedure can certify $\Gstar$.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
Common random numbers make the comparison tight but make all inference
\emph{conditional on a fixed draw table}: the replication axis is tasks
$\times$ seeds, not re-sampling of the model. Difficulty bands come from the
no-memory arm's own $\pihat$, so per-band comparisons against no memory are
inflated by regression to the mean and are descriptive only. The timeout-edge
fault of \cref{sec:results-integrity} accounts for every cross-arm
inconsistency; removing it leaves the cost and repair results unmoved but cuts
the guard-second reduction from $-39\%$ to $-18\%$
(\appref{app:sensitivity}{the supplement}), so that magnitude rests on one
fault and we do not lean on it. Typed's \emph{caught}-redundancy count is
$52\%$ censored, because a blocked round in the steered arm has no paired
no-memory draw to recover its class from; the claim in \cref{tab:main} rests on
\emph{paid} redundancy, uncensored in every arm.

\paragraph{External validity.}
Scope is the strongest limitation. \emph{One model, one temperature}: every
number is \texttt{qwen2.5-coder:7b} at $T = 1.0$, and the steering null may be
scale-dependent, since a larger model might obey an instruction a 7B ignores;
we predict, and do not test, that the guard result transfers, it being a
property of the runtime. \emph{One fault distribution, and a filtered one}:
ConDefects faults are single-file competitive-programming submissions with a
rich shipped pool, which is what makes a clean counterexample oracle possible
and is unlike a repository-scale issue, and the slow-reference filter
(\cref{sec:design}) removed the harder ones, so our cost reductions understate
and our repair rates overstate. \emph{One scale of memory}: episodes run at
most $20$ rounds and memory holds a median of one entry when the guard fires,
which is why we claim nothing for the index.

\paragraph{Statistical validity, and baselines we did not run.}
A per-cell test overstates precision, so every headline comparison is reported
at both units with a task-clustered interval (\cref{tab:main}); all keep their
sign there, with typed-versus-untyped weakening from \pval{0.007} to
\pval{0.030} as it should. Only two outcomes on three arms are confirmatory.
The dead-band steering result is the weakest positive we report---one band of
five, per-task \pval{0.047}, no formal interaction test---so we treat it as a
hypothesis. Finally, the comparison a reader will want is against a
per-modification-point prioritizer~\cite{venugopal2020modification}, the
mechanism our guard is closest to (\cref{sec:related-order}), reimplemented
here so that only the key varies. Three such arms are implemented and pass the
round-1 pairing test; none has been run at scale, which is why
\cref{tab:positioning} is argued mechanically rather than measured.

\paragraph{Reproducibility, and what ``pre-specified'' means.}
We say \emph{pre-specified} rather than pre-registered: metrics, arms,
falsifiers and the banding rule were fixed in a design document and in the
frozen corpus and protocol artifacts before the reported run, timestamped in
the archive, but there is no third-party registry entry. One chronology bears
on that: the guard correction of \cref{sec:impl} was made after inspecting an
\emph{earlier} run's accounting and \emph{before} the reported run.
